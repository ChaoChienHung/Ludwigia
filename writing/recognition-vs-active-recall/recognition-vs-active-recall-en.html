Core Metaphor: Recognizing a Rabbit vs Drawing One

  • Recognition: if you see long ears and red eyes, you may already guess that it is a rabbit
  • Generation: if I ask you to draw a rabbit, it immediately reveals whether you understand the whole structure
  • The same kind of "knowing" can show very different levels of fidelity depending on the test format

Two Modes: Recognition vs Recall

  • Recognition: you remember only after seeing the options or cues
  • Recall: you can reconstruct the idea without being given clues first, by writing, speaking, or drawing
  • A common learning gap is thinking you understand something when in fact you are only familiar with the cues

MCQ: Why Multiple Choice Often Lets You Get the Answer with Only "a Little Bit" of Understanding

  • Multiple choice reduces the search space: you only need to eliminate wrong answers; you do not necessarily need to generate the right one
  • It can easily inflate your confidence: getting the answer right does not mean you can explain it, transfer it, or create with it
  • But MCQ still has value: it is useful for quickly scanning a topic and doing low-cost review

Free Response: Why Being Able to Write or Draw It Is Much Closer to Real Understanding

  • It requires a fuller representation: the structure of the concept, the causal links, and the boundary conditions
  • It exposes blind spots: wherever you get stuck is usually the missing piece
  • It is closer to real use: real problem solving does not hand you four candidate answers to guess from

Practice Design: How to Move Yourself from Recognition to Recall

  • Mix formats: use MCQ first to scan the territory, then use short-answer prompts to force reconstruction
  • Add delayed recall: answer again the next day so you do not confuse short-term familiarity with genuine retention
  • Regenerate mistakes: turn wrong multiple-choice questions into prompts that require you to write the answer from scratch
